# Claim Register

This register tracks maturity, not truth by declaration. Final states (`retained`, `revised`, or `rejected`) remain provisional within this exploratory programme.

| ID | Claim | Commitment | Maturity | Primary pilots |

| C-002 | Semantic grouping earns causal status when it provides carrier-invariant, intervention-relevant prediction beyond lower-level groupings. | Core test | operationalized | threat-avoidance, fear-conditioning, fiat-money |
| C-003 | An estimated effective field predicts behaviour better than its component measures considered independently. | Candidate integrative construct | bounded | threat-avoidance |
| C-004 | Some recurrent meaning-sensitive feedback loops form self-maintaining attractor regimes demonstrable through perturbation, return, switching, or hysteresis. | Dynamical hypothesis | rivalled | threat-avoidance |

## C-001 — Core semantic-field loop

- **Current boundary:** The claim concerns currently actionable possibilities, not every influence on cognition.
- **Principal rival:** Separate attention, valuation, confidence, affordance, and action-selection variables suffice.
- **Falsifier:** Semantic intervention produces no coherent or predictive redistribution beyond the component variables.
- **Candidate measurements:** choices, exploration, perceived affordances, counterfactual generation, confidence, controllability, and willingness to test alternatives.
- **Evidence:** RUN-002 found instruction-sensitive fear and avoidance but retained component rivals (SRC-TA-001–004). RUN-006 found that recognized status and intrinsically worthless tokens can alter exchange possibilities, while search, coordination, trust, record-keeping, payment coupling, salience, and enforcement remain viable. The claim is operationalized by requiring status-sensitive redistribution beyond those measured components (SRC-FM-001–004).
## C-002 — Semantic causal invariance

- **Current boundary:** Semantic description must improve prediction or intervention across carrier variation.
- **Principal rival:** Lower-level stimulus features, associative history, and conditioned response fully explain the effect.
- **Falsifier:** Meaning-preserving carrier changes do not generalize, or meaning changes add no predictive information once physical and learned variables are controlled.
- **Candidate measurements:** intervention effects grouped by interpreted content versus physical carrier.
- **Evidence:** RUN-004 established a serious lower-level rival for standard Pavlovian preparations (SRC-FC-001–004). RUN-005 found causal instruction effects but also response- and circuit-specific dissociations: propositional expectancy, reinforcement history, and experience remain viable explanations, and no study crosses carrier with content or tests semantic grouping out of sample. The claim is operationalized by requiring that joint contrast beyond those measured rivals (SRC-FC-005–008).

## C-003 — Incremental value of the effective field

- **Current boundary:** The field is a candidate integrative state-space, not an assumed psychological primitive.
- **Principal rival:** A disconnected multivariate model predicts equally well with less ontological commitment.
- **Falsifier:** The field representation fails to improve out-of-sample prediction or intervention selection.
- **Candidate measurements:** comparative predictive performance, structured covariance, and intervention-induced redistribution.
- **Evidence:** RUN-003 operationalized a multivariate comparison using repeated fear, expectancy, action-value, response-availability, avoidance, and context measures. The selected studies establish dissociations and intervention-sensitive recurrence but do not fit or validate an effective-field representation against separate-component, inhibitory-learning, or occasion-setting models (SRC-TA-005–008).

## C-004 — Semantic attractor regimes

- **Current boundary:** Recurrence alone is insufficient; semantic content must participate causally in maintenance.
- **Principal rival:** Habit, reinforcement, or nonsemantic regulation explains persistence without an attractor or semantic variable.
- **Falsifier:** No perturbation recovery, switching threshold, hysteresis, or content-sensitive loop disruption is observed.
- **Candidate measurements:** displacement, recovery trajectory, intervention strength, switching threshold, and entry/exit asymmetry.
- **Evidence:** RUN-003 found return of low-cost avoidance after response restoration and reinstatement after new aversive events, but no graded displacement-and-release test, autonomous recovery trajectory, switching threshold, or entry/exit hysteresis. Inhibitory learning, retrieval competition, learned action value, and occasion setting remain sufficient rivals for the observed recurrence (SRC-TA-005–008).

## C-005 — Historical recursion

- **Current boundary:** Later generative conditions must be measurably changed by prior outputs.
- **Principal rival:** Stable background traits or environments explain both earlier outputs and later propensities.
- **Falsifier:** Controlling prior conditions removes the apparent recursive effect.
- **Candidate measurements:** longitudinal formation propensity, learned repertoires, institutionalized constraints, and counterfactual histories.
- **Evidence:** RUN-007 found an operational recursive case: commercial-bank lending creates deposits that become later purchasing and settlement conditions, while repayment extinguishes them. Balance-sheet rules, regulation, borrower demand, policy, and central-bank settlement remain serious rival conditions, so the evidence does not identify semantic feedback as the reproducing mechanism (SRC-FM-005–008).

## C-006 — Target-family unification

- **Current boundary:** Unity is claimed only where target classes constrain future trajectory selection through comparable control relations.
- **Principal rival:** Regulatory, learned, normative, and endorsed targets require unrelated explanations.
- **Falsifier:** The shared control description adds no prediction or intervention value across target classes.
- **Candidate measurements:** perturbation compensation, means substitution, temporal reach, and target revision.
- **Evidence:** RUN-006 bounded monetary targets into represented status, equilibrium acceptance, exchange expectations, settlement affordances, and enforceable obligations (SRC-FM-001–004). RUN-007 separated representation, coordination, infrastructure, enforcement, and asymmetric power; rules-in-equilibrium and the decomposed institutional model are serious rivals to a single control family. Unity now requires superior intervention prediction across typed changes in acceptance, convertibility, clearing, regulation, issuer identity, and classification (SRC-FM-005–008).

## C-007 — Typed cross-scale comparison

- **Current boundary:** Similarity of vocabulary never establishes identity of mechanism.
- **Principal rival:** Cross-scale comparison is either too weak to explain or mistakenly equivocal.
- **Falsifier:** Typing the relation fails to constrain predictions or block invalid transfers.
- **Candidate measurements:** transfer accuracy and failure patterns for each relation type.
- **Evidence:** RUN-004 separated computational-learning, mechanistic circuit, and measurement relations (SRC-FC-001–004). RUN-005 tested the constraint against instruction and experience evidence: instruction updated some behavioural and neural measures while reinforcement and experience remained distinguishable in others, so transferring one undifferentiated “fear” or semantic mechanism across levels would lose observed structure (SRC-FC-005–008).

## C-008 — Compression hypothesis

- **Current boundary:** Compactness must be earned out of sample and compared with equally complex domain-specific models.
- **Principal rival:** Domain-specific decompositions retain more predictive information and require fewer exceptions.
- **Falsifier:** A shared basis fails to compress held-out cases or grows without a predictive plateau.
- **Candidate measurements:** out-of-sample accuracy, description length, exception count, and marginal gain from basis expansion.
- **Evidence:** RUN-008 compared all three pilots with the common case protocol. A thin shared basis transferred case documentation, carrier-by-content testing, rival specification, intervention requirements, and relation typing, but domain-specific decompositions retained the only operational predictive variables and fewer substantive exceptions. No fitted, complexity-matched, or held-out compression test was performed (SRC-TA-001–008; SRC-FC-001–008; SRC-FM-001–008).

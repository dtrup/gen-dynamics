# RUN-008 Cross-Pilot Comparison

## Status and scope

Complete. This is an exploratory, bounded comparison of the 24 already selected sources; it is not an exhaustive or systematic review and adds no new evidence.

The common case protocol from the synthesis is used as a discrimination checklist, not as a presumption that all fields apply to every case.

## First-pass comparison

The protocol succeeds primarily as a **failure-prevention device**. It forces every pilot to name outcomes, rival variables, interventions, missing dynamical criteria, and the type of cross-scale relation. This exposes three recurring errors: treating instruction effects as uniquely semantic, treating recurrence as an attractor, and treating institutional recognition as sufficient for monetary operation.

The strongest shared structure is narrow: represented or learned content can alter action, but semantic causation requires content-sensitive prediction across carrier variation after domain-specific component variables are controlled. This structure is operationally transferable even though no pilot performs the decisive test.

Most other proposed fields do not compress across cases without loss. Body state is absent at the institutional level; formation propensity lacks a common estimator; state variables and operators are domain-specific; and return, barrier, and hysteresis criteria are unsupported in every pilot. The first pass therefore retains typed comparison while rejecting mechanism identity.

## Second-pass compression test

### Candidate shared basis

A minimal cross-pilot basis can encode six items without immediate equivocation:

1. a typed represented or learned content, when present;
2. domain-specific generative conditions;
3. observable action consequences;
4. feedback into later conditions;
5. a controlled or proposed intervention;
6. explicit rival variables and a typed cross-scale relation.

This basis is useful for case documentation and test design. It does not yet function as a predictive model. Its terms require different measurements in each pilot, and “content” is optional in standard Pavlovian defensive learning rather than a universal primitive.

### Shared basis versus domain decompositions

| Criterion | Candidate shared basis | Domain-specific decompositions | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Coverage | Describes all three cases at a coarse functional level | Describes all measured variables and interventions | Shared basis covers cases but omits supported detail. |
| Predictive variables | Does not supply fitted variables or held-out predictions | Provides expectancy/action variables, learning/circuit variables, or institutional balance-sheet and coordination variables | Domain decompositions have the only presently operational predictive variables. |
| Intervention selection | Requires domain controls before selecting an intervention | Distinguishes instruction, incentive, reinforcement, circuit, payment, settlement, and enforcement changes | Shared terms do not choose among interventions. |
| Exception burden | Must mark body state, semantic content, attractor fields, and several operators as absent or differently typed | Treats these differences as ordinary domain structure | Shared basis accumulates exceptions when made substantive. |
| Cross-case transfer | Transfers the carrier-by-content test and relation typing | Transfers little vocabulary but preserves mechanisms | Only methodological tests transfer cleanly. |
| Description length | Shorter at the heading level | Longer but records discriminating variables | No measured-description-length advantage has been demonstrated. |

### Decision on C-008

The three-pilot comparison supplies a serious rival to the compression hypothesis: a thin shared protocol plus domain-specific models. This hybrid is more defensible than a common basis of field dimensions and operators because it preserves the only robust transfers—carrier-by-content testing, explicit rivals, intervention requirements, and cross-scale typing—without pretending that state variables, maintenance operators, or loop topology are shared.

C-008 therefore advances from **bounded** to **rivalled**, not to operationalized or tested. A genuine compression test would require a fixed coding basis, matched complexity penalties, held-out cases, prediction or intervention outcomes, and an exception count. Those are publication-scale tasks already represented in the backlog and are not performed here.

## Cross-pilot conclusions

- **Retain as a methodological constraint:** cross-scale relation typing blocks empirically visible category errors across neural, behavioural, experiential, and institutional descriptions.
- **Retain as an operational test:** semantic causation requires carrier-by-content invariance and incremental intervention value beyond domain-specific rivals.
- **Do not infer:** recurrence is an attractor, represented content is a universal cause, or monetary recognition subsumes infrastructure and power.
- **Prefer provisionally:** a thin common protocol paired with domain-specific models over a substantive common field/operator basis.

## Residual uncertainty

- The comparison reuses a purposive 24-source corpus and is neither independent nor out of sample.
- Full-text inspection was uneven across pilots.
- No shared basis or domain model was fitted, scored, or complexity-matched.
- The common protocol may favor the synthesis vocabulary because it supplied the comparison fields.
- Fiat money lacks controlled interventions comparable to the laboratory pilots, limiting perturbation comparison.

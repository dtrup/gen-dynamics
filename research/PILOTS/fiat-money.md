# Pilot: Fiat Money as Semantic and Institutional Control

## Status

Complete. Exploratory and not publication ready.

## Scope

Test meaning-sensitive carrier variation while separating shared representation from coordination value, infrastructure, enforcement, and asymmetric power.

## Claims under test

- C-001: core semantic-field loop.
- C-002: semantic causal invariance.
- C-005: historical recursion.
- C-006: target-family unification.
- C-007: typed cross-scale comparison.

## Rival explanations

- Material incentives and enforcement explain monetary behaviour without semantic variables.
- Coordination equilibria suffice without an effective-field construct.
- Institutional persistence is mistakenly attributed to shared representation rather than infrastructure and power.

## RUN-006 carrier comparison

The bounded result is **recognized status plus carrier-specific affordances, not semantic invariance**. Searle clarifies why physically heterogeneous objects can share monetary status: collective recognition assigns powers not contained in their material composition. Camera and colleagues show that intrinsically worthless tokens can acquire exchange use and stabilize cooperation among strangers. These sources make represented status and acceptance empirically relevant candidates rather than mere descriptions of material value.

The rivals remain strong. Kiyotaki and Wright show how acceptance can arise from search frictions and equilibrium trading incentives without a distinct semantic-control variable. In the token experiment, cooperation was possible without money and token use also supplied portable record keeping and a coordination device. Raghubir and Srivastava show that cash, scrip, gift certificates, and credit-card cues alter spending through payment coupling and salience even when nominal value is matched. A shared monetary interpretation therefore does not make carriers causally interchangeable.

RUN-006 consequently rejects two shortcuts. Physical worth is not necessary for a carrier to function as money, but intrinsic worthlessness does not prove semantic causation. Conversely, common face value or monetary status does not justify ignoring material interface, payment timing, salience, learned practice, or infrastructure. C-001 becomes operationalized by requiring a change in recognized status to predict a redistribution of exchange possibilities after these components are controlled. C-002 remains only operationalized because none of the studies independently crosses interpreted status with physical carrier and compares held-out predictions.

A discriminating design would cross the same payment infrastructure and incentives with differently assigned status, and the same assigned status across cash, paper certificate, account entry, and digital token carriers. It would measure acceptance, spending, exchange partners, settlement success, perceived obligation, trust, salience, and coupling. A semantic grouping would have to generalize across carriers and improve prediction or intervention selection beyond equilibrium acceptance, transaction cost, enforcement, interface, and payment-timing models.

## RUN-007 typed institutional decomposition

The evidence does not support treating money as one semantic control mechanism. It supports five interacting but noninterchangeable relations:

1. **Representation:** a unit, token, deposit, or liability is classified as discharging a price or obligation; this is the status-content relation emphasized by institutional accounts.
2. **Coordination:** agents accept a carrier because they expect others to accept it, reducing search and trust problems; equilibrium and repeated-exchange models explain this relation.
3. **Infrastructure:** accounts, balance sheets, convertibility, clearing, and final settlement make monetary promises transferable and comparable; commercial-bank deposits and central-bank reserves occupy different operational positions.
4. **Enforcement:** taxation, regulation, contract, and eligibility for discharging public obligations make some units and liabilities consequential independently of voluntary recognition.
5. **Asymmetric power:** public authorities and regulated banks have unequal capacities to define the unit, issue accepted liabilities, require payment, constrain conversion, and supply final settlement.

These relations can reinforce one another without belonging to one causal family. Collective recognition without settlement capacity may fail; infrastructure without expected acceptance may remain unused; coordination may emerge without state fiat; state obligations can privilege a unit without equal trust in every issuer. Rules-in-equilibrium are the strongest integrative rival because they combine represented rules with incentive-compatible coordination while avoiding representation-only foundations.

RUN-007 also supplies bounded historical recursion. Commercial-bank lending creates deposits that become later purchasing and settlement conditions, while repayment extinguishes deposit money; prior institutional outputs therefore alter subsequent possibilities. But balance-sheet rules, regulation, borrower demand, policy, and central-bank settlement jointly condition that recursion. The evidence does not show that outputs reproduce the monetary regime through semantic feedback alone.

The discriminating unit for C-006 is consequently a typed intervention matrix, not a common label. Changes to public acceptance, convertibility, clearing access, regulatory eligibility, issuer identity, or shared classification should be compared for substitution, compensation, temporal reach, and distributional effects. A unified control family earns status only if those typed relations predict intervention outcomes better than this decomposed institutional model.

## Falsifiers

- Carrier-invariant interpreted value adds no prediction beyond incentives, sanctions, and coordination variables.
- Changing interpretation while preserving institutional conditions has no distinct consequence.
- Cross-scale comparison cannot state whether recurrence is semantic, functional, dynamical, or genealogical.

## Residual uncertainty

- Recognized institutional status was not experimentally manipulated independently of acceptance, incentives, or infrastructure.
- Payment-form effects demonstrate carrier sensitivity but do not identify whether interpretation, salience, coupling, or learned practice is causal.
- Laboratory tokens and bilateral search models do not reproduce taxation, legal tender, banking infrastructure, or asymmetric monetary power.
- Only two of eight selected sources were inspected in full text; state, hierarchy, and rules-in-equilibrium sources were mostly inspected at abstract or summary level.
- The decomposition identifies distinct relations but does not estimate their relative causal contribution or distributional incidence.
- Bank-money recursion is documented operationally, but semantic feedback is not isolated from accounting, regulation, demand, and settlement.
